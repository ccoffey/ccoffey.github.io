Arcade Cabinet

A two-player tabletop arcade built to play the games I loved from my childhood with my own kids

Woodworking 3D Printing Raspberry Pi RetroPie
Build Demonstration

The Build Story

My twin brother and I built an arcade machine together years ago. As far as I know, it still sits proudly in the lobby of the engineering department of NUI Maynooth, where it entertains visitors and collects money (20c a game) for the engineering club we used to belong to.

I wanted to build another one, but a much smaller, sleeker one this time. The unit in the engineering department was a massive, full-size and very rectangular cabinet housing a desktop PC that had to be weighed down with sandbags so it wouldn't get knocked over by over-eager students. This time, I just wanted to build from the control deck up and either wall-mount it in my kids' playroom or sit it on top of an existing table.

I framed the internal skeleton from 2×2 planed white deal timber and skinned it with MDF. To create the exterior contours, I skinned the cabinet again with 3D-printed PLA panels—piecing them together like a giant jigsaw puzzle because my Prusa MK3S+ build plate is approximately 200×200 mm. The joysticks and buttons were actually a leftover set from that first college build; like so many other things, they had been patiently waiting in my attic for a new purpose.

To eliminate the seams, I filled the joints with Isopon P38 body filler (typically used in car repair, a first for me), followed by endless rounds of sanding, spraying high-build primer (another first for me), and finishing with leftover paint from my attic that we used on the doors under our stairs last year.

The electronics were intentionally kept straightforward: a Raspberry Pi 5 running RetroPie with an external hard drive and a cheap set of speakers. To make the cabinet truly special, I bought and disassembled USB adapters for original Nintendo 64 and GameCube controllers, integrating the ports directly into the cabinet so I could play the games I loved growing up together with my own children, using my original childhood controllers.

One of the hardest parts of the project was getting the joystick positions and button ergonomics correct, requiring multiple drilling templates and extensive trial and error. The control panel will eventually be wrapped in a custom vinyl graphic printed by Gorilla Graphics (gorillagraphics.ie). I am still tweaking the final design.

Next Steps & Planned Upgrades

  • Wrap control panel in custom vinyl graphic artwork from gorillagraphics.ie
  • Install GameCube controller ports and holders
  • Finish fine detailing around the monitor cutout
  • Still a few visible seams that I am not happy about
